A funnel counts people who reached its first step, then checks their later actions in order. It does not count every occurrence as a new attempt.

## Read the report

1. Open a funnel under **Observe → Funnels**.
2. Select the date range for entry into the first step.
3. Use **Complete within** to test a conversion window and **Analyze users** to select a saved Segment or **All users**.
4. Review **Conversion**, **Started**, and **Completed**.
5. Use **Conversion** for the step breakdown or **Trend** for conversion by entry day.
6. Open a step name or user count to inspect the people who reached it.

Changing **Complete within** in the report changes this analysis; edit the funnel if you want to change its saved default.

## Worked example

Illustrative counts: 100 people start, 60 reach project creation, and 30 invite a teammate.

| Measurement | Calculation | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overall conversion | 30 final completers ÷ 100 starters | 30% |
| Project-creation step conversion | 60 ÷ 100 | 60% |
| Invitation step conversion | 30 ÷ 60 | 50% |
| Dropped after project creation | 60 − 30 | 30 people |

**Largest drop** compares the percentage lost between adjacent steps. It does not establish why people stopped.

## Timing rules that matter

- Each person starts from their earliest first-step occurrence within the selected entry period.
- A later step must have a strictly later timestamp than the preceding step and fit within the window measured from that first occurrence.
- Later steps can fall after the selected period ends if they still fit inside that conversion window.
- A missing intermediate step prevents later steps from counting. A second attempt is not automatically a fresh funnel entry in the same analysis.
- Recent starters may still have time to finish. Their current drop-off is not necessarily final abandonment.

The Segment filter uses current membership. Anonymous and identified activity are counted according to the identity recorded with the event.
